# Brief: Freight AI, which side first

**Decision:** Which side of ro-ro freight booking email to start Freight AI on, in the next six months.

- **A. Hauliers first, sell before building.** A "booking cockpit" for small and mid-size hauliers: send requests to several operators, parse replies, one status view. £50/mo (up to 10 trailers), £200/mo (up to 50). Phase 1: 20 conversations, 2 free pilots.
- **B. Operators first.** The planner assistant in the build spec: extract booking emails, validate against ro-ro rules (cut-offs, lane metres, accompanied/unaccompanied, IMDG class and segregation, reefer plugs), draft the reply, push via an adapter. £2,000 to £5,000/mo per operator.
- **C. Conversations only, no build yet.** Talk to 20+ hauliers and operators and collect letters of intent before writing code.
- **D. Don't do it.** Keep the job, no business.

**The business:** Pre-revenue, solo founder with hands-on ro-ro booking experience. A route desk handles about 400 emails a day with 5 to 6 planners. The build spec is written for the operator product (B), while the business plan says start with hauliers (A). Prototype uses synthetic data only, on personal hardware.

**Resources:** 20+ hours a week alongside a full-time job at a ferry operator, kept until revenue replaces it. Year-one costs about £2,000 plus a £1,000 endorsement fee. Self-funded (assumed: savings cover this comfortably).

**Success:** Within six months, a working product, written statements from buyers, and at least one pilot or paying customer, strong enough for an Innovator Founder endorsement. Year 1 target £15k to £20k.

**Lean:** Not sure. The plan argues hauliers decide fast; the founder's domain edge and the bigger cheques are on the operator side.

**Watch-outs:** Employer conflict of interest (IP and outside-business clauses not yet reviewed). Hazardous-goods errors are a safety matter. Visa position is a Skilled Worker dependant (assumed: trading is allowed, to confirm with an adviser).
